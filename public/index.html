Abstract

Adversarial machine learning (AML) investigates vulnerabilities that cause machine learning systems to produce predictions deviating from human expectations. Emerging paradigms—including backdoor attacks (at pre-training, training, inference stages), weight attacks (at post-training, deployment, inference stages), and adversarial example attacks (at inference stage)—exploit these vulnerabilities across the machine learning lifecycle. Despite their shared adversarial objectives, current research efforts remain fragmented, lacking a unified perspective to guide systematic understanding.

This work bridges this gap through three key contributions: (1) a lifecycle-aware mathematical framework unifying definitions of AML threats, (2) a hierarchical taxonomy categorizing attack methodologies while clarifying inter-paradigm relationships, and (3) extended analysis of AML in generative models and beneficial applications.

Our findings highlight the urgent need for robust security mechanisms as adversarial capabilities increasingly threaten safety-critical systems. By revealing connections between attacks across development stages, we emphasize that isolated defense strategies inevitably fail against coordinated multi-stage attacks. The research community must prioritize holistic defense strategies incorporating lifecycle-aware monitoring, adaptive hardening techniques, and unified threat models. This survey provides both theoretical foundations and practical guidelines to advance secure machine learning ecosystems.
